Sports Tech · AIoT
“If athletic training happens in a controlled environment, why can't injury risk also be monitored earlier? Predict → Prevent → Protect training time.”
Problem & Field Discovery

Competitive athletes lose weeks of critical training to preventable musculoskeletal injuries caused by unnoticed fatigue accumulation. Engaged a local basketball club (full 10-player roster), head coach, team physiotherapist, and medical doctors from RMC to uncover on-court operational realities: players reject restrictive body attachments, coaches demand zero session-time disruption, and physios require actionable kinetic telemetry rather than raw sensor waveforms.

Field Validation Evidence

Tested 3 active basketball players in live court drills and scrimmage sessions, capturing 10+ hours of continuous multimodal telemetry (ECG, EMG, IMU motion dynamics, heart rate, pressure, temperature, and SpO2) across synchronized dual ESP32 wearable nodes.

Key Product Decisions (Field Feedback → Execution)
Initial Hypothesis (From) Product Pivot (To) Strategic Rationale
Chest-only strap hardware Modular Chest + Wrist System Drastically reduced motion artifacts during sprint & jump drills while decoupling upper-body telemetry.
Direct-to-Athlete user focus Coaches + Physios + Athletes Athletes play; coaches manage load; physios interpret fatigue. Enabled real-time bench rotation decisions.
General injury prediction Target-body kinetic analysis Muscle fatigue is localized (e.g. calf/hamstring strain). Targeted EMG telemetry isolates micro-tears.
Pre-injury prevention only Prediction + Rehabilitation Tracking Extended user lifetime value: physios use identical telemetry to monitor recovery thresholds during return-to-play.
Iteration Timeline (Field Discovery → Rapid Prototyping)

Custom NOT Gate Macro

Physical Design · SkyWater 130nm CMOS Process

Implemented a custom NOT gate macro using the SkyWater 130nm CMOS process. The design was constrained to an absolute die area of 30×30 microns with a deterministic pin-ordering (West-In, East-Out) to facilitate future hierarchical integration. The flow was executed via OpenLane 2, utilizing Yosys for logic mapping and OpenROAD for detailed routing, resulting in a DRC-clean GDSII layout.

5-Stage Pipelined RISC-V Processor (RV32I)

CPU Architecture · Verilog HDL

Architected and implemented a 32-bit RISC-V CPU core supporting the RV32I base instruction set in Verilog HDL.
